# Direction decision log: the Settings page

## Grouping and structure (11 Nov 2026)
- The brewer, relayed by the brew-day design agent: "I'd like the settings to be intelligently sorted and presented in groups by function. Right now, they are a confusing long list in no particular order - maybe alphabetical?" Measured on the simulator: it is alphabetical by key, capitals first (Python sort_keys), with a setting added at run time at the end (BREWDAY_TIMER_NAMES), 51 rows, about 4 screens at 1920×1080 and 8 on a phone.
- Asked the brewer, one at a time: how he uses Settings ("Yes, that's about right": now and then, at the desk, usually one setting he knows); which look ("Brew-day look, with its title bar and tabs"); names ("Plain name first, key underneath (Recommended)").
- Read first, per his standing rule:
  - NN/g "10 Usability Heuristics": match the real world and its natural order (#2), recognition over recall (#6), consistency (#4), visibility of status (#1).
  - NN/g "Memory Recognition and Recall": context and associations make recall easier; groups and visible names give the context.
  - NN/g "Proximity Principle in Visual Design": related items close, unrelated apart; far-away items look unrelated and get missed (today's value sits ~1100 px from its name at 1920 wide).
  - NN/g "Progressive Disclosure", "Accordions on Desktop", "Tabs, Used Right": tabs only for few groups whose content needn't be compared; accordions hide content from find-in-page. Nine groups on one scrolling pane with an index keeps everything findable.
  - NN/g "Toggle-Switch Guidelines": a switch should act at once; in a form saved with a button, use other controls. So yes/no settings are two-half YES | NO controls, not switches.
  - W3C WAI "Grouping Controls": group visually and in code (fieldset/legend or role=group with a label), keep legends short and labels self-explanatory.
  - MIT 6.831 L2 Learnability (speak the user's language; jargon only for domain experts; accept synonyms and aliases in search, use one name when the interface speaks), L3 Visibility (information scent: name categories by what's in them, not "General" or "Tools"; an item that fits two places gets a see-also link), L4 Efficiency (keep paths short, anticipation), L5 Errors (prefer undo to confirmation; protect unsaved work), L18 Graphic design (white space and proximity for grouping, labels in the left margin, controls aligned in one column, no crowding).
  - Georgia Tech CS 6750, lesson 2.5 (public notes): Constantine and Lockwood's structure principle, "putting related things together and separating unrelated things, differentiating dissimilar things and making similar things resemble one another"; constraints prevent errors.
  - Norman, The Design of Everyday Things (2013), chapters 1 and 3: mapping, grouping and proximity of controls, knowledge in the world.
  - Interaction Design (id-book.com) chapters 3 and 4: conceptual models; memory and attention, recognition and categories.
- Seven structures, ordered by resonance before the roll: 1 index and one scrolling page; 2 one group at a time; 3 read-then-edit sheet; 4 wall of plates; 5 search first; 6 groups in brewing order; 7 collapsed groups with summaries. Impeccable's concept seed (021df8ab) assigned 6. Its staging challengers (navigating by zoom, a run of one-at-a-time verdicts, a time lattice) fit a one-setting-at-the-desk task poorly; none beat it.
- Groups in brewing order, written out in proposal/GROUPING.md; the brewer: "Looks right, build the mockups."
- Placement calls: Mash tun and Boil kettle in Brewhouse (the brew-day screen and the HLT pump use them to tell the kettles apart), with a see-also line in Boil; HLT pump with heat in Mash and sparge (it runs until the sparge); the override switch in Brewhouse (it describes the rig's wiring).
- Facts checked in the code: blank step types import as MashInStep, MashStep, SpargeStep, BoilStep and WaitStep; steps_mashout only adds a "Lautering" stop when set to NotificationStep; the CSV log size and count apply from the next restart; turning CSV or InfluxDB logging on needs a restart; MQTTUpdate needs a restart; BoilAutoTimer, the lid alert text and switch, CONFIRM_BEFORE_BOIL and HLT_PUMP_WITH_HEAT act while brewing.

The name promises exactly what the server does, in words the HLT shows on the brew-day screen while a logic heats it (AUTO, or the dashed HERMS button), and "heating by hand" is that screen's phrase for MANUAL with the element on (NN/g's match with the real world, and consistency). The reason is the brewer's own, which makes the rule easy to remember. Weighed: a shorter name, "Start the HLT pump when AUTO heats the HLT", needed the help to name the HERMS and came to 5 lines even at 1920. Leaving out the restart sentence saved a line, but someone who stops an unprimed pump should know the stop isn't for good.
- Measured: 4 help lines at 1920 and 1024, 5 at 1366, 8 at 768×1024 and 7 on a phone, as long as the longest help already there at each size. Checked: all 52 recorded pages differ from 20 Nov's only by the new words. Nothing overflows at seven sizes.
